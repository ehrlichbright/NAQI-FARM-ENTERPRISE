Buying vegetable oil by the litre in Lagos: how it saves you money

By NAQI FARM ENTERPRISE · 13 October 2026 · 5 min read

Buying vegetable oil by the litre means you bring your own keg, drum or container, the seller fills it from bulk stock, and you pay only for the oil that goes in. You skip the cost of the bottle, the label and the markup that every reseller adds on top. For anyone who cooks every day or sells oil for a living, that difference adds up quickly.

Why does vegetable oil keep getting more expensive in Lagos?

Vegetable oil prices in Lagos move with input costs and supply, and in 2026 they have mostly moved up. A May 2026 survey of Lagos markets reported by Nairametrics found the 25-litre gallon of local vegetable oil rising from about N53,000 to N62,000, with traders pointing to input costs and tight supply.

You can't control the market price of oil. But you can control how much extra you pay on top of it, and that is where packaging comes in.

What are you actually paying for in a bottle of oil?

When you buy a sealed bottle or a branded gallon, the price covers more than the oil inside. It also pays for:

  • The container itself, which you often throw away or reuse for something else.
  • The label and printing on the outside.
  • Handling at every step, because each distributor, wholesaler and retailer between the factory and you adds a margin.

None of those things go into your stew. So when you buy oil in bulk and bring your own container, you keep that money instead.

How does buying oil by the litre work?

At NAQI FARM ENTERPRISE, it works in three steps:

  1. Bring your container. A keg, a drum, or any clean container with a good lid.
  2. Watch the meter. We fill it through our metering system in front of you, so you can see each litre as it goes in.
  3. Pay for exactly that. The meter reading is what you pay for. Nothing more.

Our oil is Olein refined vegetable oil, popularly known as Kings oil, and our tanker brings it straight from the manufacturer to our depot in Mushin. There is no middleman between the factory and our tank, which is the other half of why the price stays fair. If you want to know more about the oil itself, read our plain guide to Olein oil.

Who benefits most from buying oil in bulk?

Anyone who uses a lot of oil, regularly, gets the most from buying by the litre:

  • Market traders who buy at depot level and resell in smaller quantities, so their margin grows.
  • Restaurants, bukas and caterers who fry every day and go through oil fast.
  • Hotels that need a steady supply for their kitchens.
  • Families who would rather fill one keg every few weeks than buy small bottles at shop prices.

What should you check before buying oil by the litre?

Buying loose oil only saves you money if the oil is genuine and the measurement is honest, so check these four things wherever you buy:

  1. Can you see the measurement? A meter you can watch is better than "trust me, it's full".
  2. Where does the oil come from? Ask whether it comes directly from the manufacturer or has passed through several hands.
  3. Is your container clean and dry? Water or old residue in a keg spoils good oil. Wash, dry and cover it before you come.
  4. Is delivery priced separately? A clear oil price plus a clear delivery charge is easier to compare than one mixed-up figure.

Frequently asked questions

Is buying vegetable oil by the litre cheaper than buying bottles?

Usually, yes, because you are not paying for the bottle, the label or the markups added at each step of resale. You pay only for the oil measured into your own container.

What container should I bring to buy oil by the litre?

Bring a clean, dry keg, drum or container with a lid that closes well. Any size works, because you pay for the litres measured, not the size of the container.
